When the user asks for something explained simply (they're "not a coder/mathematician," feeling overloaded, etc.), the fix is to **define specific coding/technical terms in plain language inline**, not to drop the whole explanation to a childlike level with kindergarten analogies.

**Why:** Explicit correction (2026-08-19): "don't explain it like [I'm] five... I'm smart, I have a very good vocabulary, it's just that I'm not the coder... some [terms are] quite specific, especially coding terms." The gap is *specialized vocabulary they haven't happened to encounter* (units mismatch in a scalarized objective function, NSGA-II vs weighted-sum scalarization, etc.), not general reasoning ability.

**How to apply:** Keep sentence complexity, structure, and inferential leaps at a normal intelligent-adult level. When a coding/domain-specific term comes up, define it briefly the first time (as you would for any specialized vocabulary a smart non-expert wouldn't know), then keep using the real term rather than a watered-down substitute throughout. Avoid over-the-top analogies (mixing desks, kilograms-vs-minutes) as the default register — reserve them for cases that genuinely need it, not as a blanket style.
